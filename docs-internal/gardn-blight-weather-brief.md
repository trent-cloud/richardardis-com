# Blight Weather — Gardn social film

Production brief. Prepared 17 August 2026.

A 38-second vertical film in which an app saves a tomato crop by sending exactly
one message. Built to be generated in Palmier Pro for about £50, and to be posted
while the threat it describes is actually happening.

| | |
|---|---|
| Runtime | 38 sec |
| Format | 9:16, native |
| Generations | ~47 takes across 14 setups |
| Budget | £50 (~8–12,000 Palmier credits) |

## 01 · Why this isn't the frost film

The obvious Gardn film is *Frost Tonight* — it's the example on the site, and
it's the best story the product has.

It's also two months early. It is 17 August. A UK gardener watching a frost film
this week feels nothing, because frost isn't a threat they have. The whole
premise of Gardn is that it knows what matters *this week, in your postcode* — so
a film that's out of season quietly argues against the product.

What is happening in a UK garden right now is late blight. Two consecutive warm,
humid nights and outdoor tomatoes are gone inside four days. It's weather-driven,
postcode-specific, and it has a real save: pick everything green and ripen it
indoors. It's the same film structure with the stakes moved into August.

**Frost Tonight is the October post.** The shot list below is a template — swap
the threat, the act and the payoff, keep beats 1, 2 and 6 verbatim, and it
re-shoots for a second £50 in autumn.

### Check before you generate

This film asserts that Gardn sends a blight warning. If it doesn't, the ad is a
promise the app breaks on install, and that shows up in store reviews within a
week. If blight alerts aren't real yet, use the drop-in swap in section 05 — it
makes the same film out of heat and holiday, which any weather-aware app
genuinely does.

## 02 · The film

Six beats. No dialogue, no faces in close-up, no generated text. Captions are
burned in at the edit — 85% of this will be watched muted.

### Beat 1 — Hook · 00:00–00:02

Threat in frame one, in motion. Nothing here explains the app — it earns the next
two seconds and nothing else.

**S1 — hero · 8 takes**

```
Extreme macro, vertical 9:16. A green tomato on the vine. A greasy brown-black
lesion spreads across its skin in accelerated time, edges feathering outward,
water droplets trembling on the surface. Flat overcast light, humid grey-green.
Slow push in. Shallow depth of field, 100mm macro. No text, no logos, no people.
```

Caption in at 0:00, hard cut, no fade: **"Your tomatoes have about 72 hours."**

**S1-b — alt hook · 3 takes**

```
Extreme macro, vertical 9:16. Underside of a tomato leaf, a brown patch ringed
with fine white downy mould creeping along the veins. Heavy rain droplets rolling
off the edge. Dim humid light. Slow drift. No text, no logos.
```

**S1-c — alt hook · 3 takes**

```
Low wide, vertical 9:16. A small domestic greenhouse in heavy grey August rain,
water sheeting down the glass, tomato plants dim and green inside. Static camera,
rain moving. Overcast, no sun. No text, no logos, no people.
```

### Beat 2 — The message · 00:02–00:07

The only time the product appears. Generate the phone with a *blank glowing
screen* and composite the real Gardn UI in the timeline — see trap 01.

**S2 · 3 takes**

```
Vertical 9:16, from inside a dark greenhouse looking out at rain. A phone lies
face-up on a weathered potting bench among seed trays and twine. The screen glows
plain warm white, blank, no icons, no writing. Rain streaking the glass behind.
Slow push in. No text, no logos.
```

**S3 · 4 takes**

```
Vertical 9:16, over the shoulder. A phone held in a gardener's soil-stained hand,
screen glowing plain warm white and completely blank. Background soft: green
foliage, rain. Handheld, slight natural drift. Shallow focus on the phone.
No text, no icons, no logos.
```

Composite over the blank screen: *"Blight weather in your postcode — two humid
nights running. Pick every tomato today, even the green ones. They'll ripen
indoors."*

### Beat 3 — The act · 00:07–00:17

Fast, unglamorous, urgent. This is the longest beat and the cheapest — these are
ordinary shots the model handles well.

**S4 · 3 takes**

```
Vertical 9:16. Hands picking green and faintly blushing tomatoes from the vine at
speed, dropping them into a wooden trug. Rain drumming on a greenhouse roof
above. Motion blur on the hands. Humid grey light. No text, no faces, no logos.
```

**S5 · 2 takes**

```
Vertical 9:16, low angle looking up into tomato foliage. A wooden trug filling
with green tomatoes, hands moving in and out of frame above. Dim green light
through wet leaves. Handheld. No text, no logos.
```

**S6 · 2 takes**

```
Vertical 9:16. A heaped trug of green tomatoes carried through a kitchen doorway
from the rain into warm interior light. Following camera, slight bounce. Wet
shoulders, steam. No faces, no text, no logos.
```

**S7 · 2 takes**

```
Vertical 9:16. Rows of green tomatoes laid out on newspaper along a sunlit
kitchen windowsill. Slow lateral track along the row. Soft morning light, dust in
the air. Domestic, lived-in. No text, no logos.
```

### Beat 4 — What would have happened · 00:17–00:24

The counterfactual. This beat is what makes the film worth sharing rather than
just watching — cut hard on the beat change, no dissolve in.

**S8 · 4 takes**

```
Vertical 9:16, static wide, time-lapse over four days. An outdoor tomato plant
collapsing: leaves curling and browning, stems blackening, fruit going soft and
dark on the vine. Grey overcast light shifting day to night. Locked-off camera.
No text, no people, no logos.
```

Caption: **"The plants went in four days."**

**S9 · 3 takes**

```
Extreme macro time-lapse, vertical 9:16. A dark lesion consuming a tomato, skin
dulling and wrinkling, white mould creeping at the margin. Cold flat light.
Locked-off. No text, no logos.
```

### Beat 5 — Payoff · 00:24–00:32

Warmth arrives for the first time. Everything before this is grey.

**S10 · 3 takes**

```
Vertical 9:16, time-lapse on a kitchen windowsill. Green tomatoes slowly blushing
to deep red over days, hard sunlight sweeping across them and fading, again and
again. Locked-off camera. Warm domestic light. No text, no logos.
```

Caption: **"The tomatoes didn't."**

**S11 · 3 takes**

```
Vertical 9:16, macro. A hand lifting one ripe red tomato from a sunlit windowsill
row. Warm low-angle light, soft shadows. Slow, unhurried. No faces, no text,
no logos.
```

**S12 · 2 takes**

```
Vertical 9:16, macro. A ripe tomato halved on a wooden board, seeds and juice
glistening, backlit by a low sun. Very shallow depth of field, slow push in.
No text, no logos.
```

### Beat 6 — The turn · 00:32–00:38

The actual positioning, and the part people quote. Every other app in this
category sells you more notifications. This one sells you fewer.

**S13 · 2 takes**

```
Vertical 9:16. A phone lying dark and silent on a sunlit kitchen table beside a
bowl of red tomatoes. Nothing happens. A fly drifts through the light. Static
camera, long still hold. Warm quiet domestic afternoon. No text, no logos.
```

Captions: **"Gardn didn't message again for nine days."** /
**"It only speaks when it matters."**

**End card · 0 credits.** Gardn wordmark, "Grow together.", App Store and Google
Play badges. Built as a still overlay on the tail of S13 — never generated.
Hold 1.5 sec.

## 03 · Where the £50 goes

Palmier Pro is $29/mo for 5,000 credits, roughly 2–7 minutes of generated video
depending on model. £50 lands around 8–12,000 credits — call it four minutes of
generation you can actually trust.

The allocation is deliberately lopsided. The instinct is to spread credit evenly
across fifteen shots; that's how you end up with a competent film nobody watches.
Retention on a vertical social film is decided in the first two seconds, so **S1
gets eight takes and most shots get two.** If you run short, cut takes from the
middle beats — a slightly weaker picking shot costs nothing, a weak hook costs
the whole film.

| Beat | Shots | Takes | Approx. share |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Hook | S1, S1-b, S1-c | 14 | 37% |
| The message | S2, S3 | 7 | 18% |
| The act | S4–S7 | 9 | 24% |
| Counterfactual | S8, S9 | 7 | 18% |
| Payoff | S10–S12 | 8 | 21% |
| The turn | S13 | 2 | 5% |
| End card | overlay | 0 | — |
| *Held back for hook re-rolls* | — | — | *25%* |
| **Generated** | ~3.8 min raw → 38 sec cut | **47** | **100%** |

Do not spend the reserve on new shots. Spend it re-rolling S1 after you've seen
the assembly, because you'll know by then exactly what the hook has to do.

## 04 · Seven ways to waste the credit

Every one of these costs real money in a generative pipeline rather than just
time.

1. **Never generate the app screen.** Video models produce garbled pseudo-text on
   phone screens, and it's unfixable. Generate a blank warm-white glow, composite
   a real Gardn screen recording over it in the timeline. Same for the wordmark
   and store badges — overlays, zero credits, perfectly legible.
2. **Don't animate the decay if it fights you.** Models resist depicting rot and
   collapse; S8 and S9 are the likeliest to burn takes. If three attempts fail,
   generate the *end state* — an already-blackened plant — and cross-dissolve
   from the healthy shot instead. Cheaper, and it cuts harder.
3. **Lock continuity with a reference image first.** Generate one hero still of
   the garden and one of the trug, then use them as reference across every shot.
   Without it you get thirteen different gardens, and no grading rescues that.
4. **Hands are the failure mode.** S4 and S11 are fingers-on-fruit, where models
   still break. Favour motion blur, partial framing, hands entering from the
   edge. Budget the extra takes there, not elsewhere.
5. **Generate 9:16 natively.** Don't make it 16:9 and crop — you lose resolution
   and every composition ends up centred and dead.
6. **Burn captions in the editor, not the prompt.** Every prompt above ends in
   "no text" for this reason. Typeset captions are legible, on-brand and free.
7. **Don't grade it warm throughout.** Beats 1–4 stay grey, humid, desaturated.
   Warmth arrives only at 00:24. If the whole film is golden-hour, the payoff has
   nothing to land against.

## 05 · The safe swap

If Gardn doesn't actually send blight warnings, this converts the film to a
threat any weather-aware app genuinely handles — three shots and two captions
change, everything else holds.

- **Threat** — ten days of August heat while you're away. S1 becomes a macro of a
  leaf curling and crisping in hard sun, soil cracking at the base of a pot.
- **Message** — "You're away until Sunday. It hasn't rained in nine days and your
  pots have about three. Ask someone for twenty minutes on Thursday."
- **The act** — a neighbour with a watering can and a key. S4–S7 become someone
  else's hands in your garden.
- **Counterfactual** — the pots nobody watered, collapsed and brown.
- **Hook caption** — "Your garden has about three days without you."
- **The turn** — unchanged. Beat 6 is the brand and it works under either threat.

## 06 · Posting it

The film is half the job. For a niche utility app these choices move downloads
more than another £50 of generation would.

- **Cut three, not one.** The 38-second master, a 12-second version (hook →
  collapse → ripening → end card) for cold reach, and a 6-second loop of S1 alone
  for paid testing.
- **Test hooks, not films.** Post the same body under S1, S1-b and S1-c.
  Whichever holds viewers past three seconds gets the money behind it. Highest
  leverage item here, and it costs nothing but scheduling.
- **Go deep before broad.** A blight PSA posted into r/GardeningUK, allotment
  Facebook groups and gardening TikTok will outperform general reach for installs,
  because it reaches people who already own tomatoes. Post it as a warning that
  happens to be made by an app, not as an ad.
- **Swap the audio at post time.** Captions are burned in, so on TikTok you can
  drop a trending sound over the top without re-exporting. Keep the ambient mix
  on Reels and Shorts.
- **Answer every comment in the first hour.** Free, and the strongest ranking
  signal available. Expect "does it work for [country]" — have the 20-market
  answer ready.
- **Match the store page to the film.** If someone lands on a first screenshot
  saying something different from what they just watched, the click is wasted.
  The blight alert should be the first screenshot while this film runs.
- **Timing.** Post on a genuinely humid week. This film is an argument that Gardn
  knows what week it is; publishing it in the wrong weather refutes its own
  premise.

## 07 · Limits of this brief

Palmier has an MCP server — it's built for Claude Code, Codex and Cursor — but it
isn't connected to the session that produced this, so no credit was spent and
nothing was generated. Everything above is ready to paste in by hand.

The better route: connect Palmier's MCP to a local Claude Code session and hand
it this shot list. It can run the generations, hold the take counts and keep the
reserve — a more honest demo of the Woodlark pattern than a film about it.

Two further limits. gardn.world could not be reached directly (blocked by the
network egress proxy), so product details here come from search results and the
richardardis.com copy — check the tone against the real app. And nothing makes a
film go viral on commission. What this one is engineered for is a high hook rate,
one quotable line, and a niche that recognises the problem on sight. That part is
controllable.
